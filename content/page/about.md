My job title is _Senior analyst_, which means both a lot and very little at the same time.

I am a curious software engineer working at the [European Commission] in Brussels. My work has gradually moved from
application development towards the larger questions surrounding software engineering: how to build systems that are
understandable, maintainable, reproducible and reliable over time.

20 years after completing my bachelor's degree in Computer Science, I returned to [UMons University] during the Covid
pandemic to pursue a master's degree in the same field. I completed it in 2024 with a thesis on [Reproducibility in
Software Engineering]. I now also contribute to teaching and knowledge-sharing around software deployment, tooling and
reproducibility. Being able to pass on things I have learnt has become one of the most rewarding parts of my work.

Reproducibility is more than a technical preference for me. It is a practical way to make software easier to trust,
operate, audit and hand over to someone else. The same principle applies to documentation, open source and good
engineering practices: make assumptions explicit, reduce unnecessary complexity and leave behind something that others
can understand and improve.

I consider myself a rational thinker with a Bayesian outlook: I usually form my views based on the available evidence
and readily update them when new facts emerge. When I encounter something unfamiliar, I tend to favour [Occam's razor],
starting with the explanation that requires the fewest assumptions while recognising that the simplest explanation is
not necessarily the correct one. When presented with an unfamiliar claim, I usually ask what evidence supports it, not
out of distrust, but out of intellectual curiosity and a genuine desire to understand. I value factual, verifiable
information while remaining mindful of uncertainty, assumptions and the possibility that I may be wrong. At the same
time, I do not judge people by their personal beliefs, identities, orientations or even their choice of keyboard layout.

2 of my superpowers are anosmia and hyperacusis: I cannot smell a thing, but I can hear noises that nobody else seems
remotely bothered by. The former can be remarkably useful in many situations, especially on public transport, while the
latter can be rather overwhelming... especially on public transport ! Mother Nature clearly assembled this sensory
package without much of a plan, but one learns to work with what one is given ! Add an [INTP-A] personality type, a
healthy appreciation for sarcasm and a tendency to overthink things, and you get a reasonably accurate picture of how I
experience the world.

As a Linux and FreeBSD enthusiast, I have worked as a full-stack developer with over a decade of experience in PHP. My
journey with Drupal began as a hobby, and I progressed to being a [contributor], service provider, and eventually, a
core maintainer.

In 2019, after careful consideration and a desire for new challenges, I [decided to resign as a Drupal core maintainer]
and shifted my focus towards Symfony and framework-independent packages. That transition taught me a lot about
abstractions, interfaces and the value of building software that can be reused outside the context in which it was first
created.

My passion for functional programming led me to become an avid user and contributor to [Nix and NixOS]. Nix gives me a
way to explore reproducible and declarative systems in practice, while also raising interesting questions about
packaging, maintenance, dependency management and digital sovereignty. Being a [NixOS committer] is both a privilege and
a continuous education in large-scale open-source maintenance.

I am particularly interested in systems that favour explicitness over magic, composition over hidden behaviour and
boring predictability over fashionable cleverness. This applies to tools, infrastructure and organisations alike. When
something fails, I usually want to understand not only what broke, but also which assumptions and incentives made the
failure likely in the first place.

If you are interested in following my work or staying up-to-date with my latest projects and contributions, I invite you
to check out my profiles on [Packagist], [Drupol's Github], [Loophp's Github] and [code.europa.eu]. These platforms
showcase my commitment to developing high-quality, versatile software solutions and my ongoing involvement in the
open-source community, where collaboration and knowledge-sharing are highly valued.

My love for {{< abbr title="Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics">}}STEM{{</abbr>}} is rooted in a deep
fascination with mathematics, especially numbers and the ways nature uses them. Data visualisation is another passion of
mine, with some of my favourite work coming from [Jason Davies] and [Mike Bostock]. I also hold great admiration for the
late [Nick Berry's blog].

I envision my ideal workplace as one that fosters **open-source projects** and is filled with **passionate**,
**open-minded**, **non-paranoid** and **friendly** teammates. Technical excellence matters to me, but so do clear
communication, humility and the willingness to explain a decision rather than merely announce it. The healthiest
communities I have encountered are practical, welcoming and able to disagree without turning every disagreement into a
question of character. If I could start my career again, I would probably spend even more time studying mathematics and
computer science, and would try to work in a university. It is a reminder that academia still has much to contribute to
the professional world.

Outside work and the geek stuff, I am fulfilling a childhood dream by learning music and playing the piano. I enjoy
photography and taught myself most of what I know about reflex cameras, just for fun. I swim a lot and enjoy riding my
mountain bike. Cycling is one of the reasons I have been an avid user and contributor to [OpenStreetMap] for more than a
decade. When I am not at work, I can often be found wandering around at home in joggers and an old T-shirt.

Finally, there is my cat _Apollo_, and the memory of the late _Izumi_ and _Nakano_. Izumi even has a mention in Drupal
core, thanks to [a patch] I submitted many years ago.
